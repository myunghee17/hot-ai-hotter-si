Huawei and Qualcomm Sign Broad Patent Deal Covering AI and Chipmaking Tech

$6.9B+ expected licensing value AI · 5G · compute · networking First Huawei–Qualcomm 5G deal Subject to FTC review

OpenAI and Synopsys Will Teach AI to Design Chips — Synopsys Pops 13% (Announced Sept 30)

SNPS +12.8% in one session $1B Amazon deal, same week FY27 outlook → $11–11.2B revenue

On September 30, Synopsys and OpenAI announced a multi-year agreement to jointly develop GPT-Synopsys, a specialized AI model trained to operate Synopsys' electronic design automation (EDA) tools — the software engineers use to design semiconductors. The vision: engineers set design objectives (power, performance, area optimization; timing and verification closure) and AI agents run the tools, interpret results, implement changes, and iterate toward verified outcomes for human review. OpenAI pays a training-subscription fee to license Synopsys' tools, and the two share revenue based on how much the model improves a customer's chip design. Customer design data is excluded from training, encrypted, with audit and retention controls. Early technology engagements with leading semiconductor customers are underway.

The same week, Synopsys disclosed a separate $1 billion multi-year agreement with Amazon for custom chips, AI products and cloud infrastructure — and raised its fiscal 2027 revenue outlook to $11–11.2 billion (from a $9.715 billion FY2026 midpoint) with non-GAAP EPS guided to $19.04–19.12. Shares jumped 12.78% to $490.54 on Thursday. If the model works, the chip-design bottleneck itself becomes an AI workload — a compounding loop the whole semiconductor industry will have to answer.

02  ·  Interconnect Startup

Volantis Raises $88 Million to Connect AI Chips with Light

San Francisco startup Volantis raised $88 million to attack the fundamental limit of AI hardware: the tiny electrical wires linking compute chips to memory. Even NVIDIA's best GPUs can hold only eight high-bandwidth memory chips because of the wires' limited reach. Volantis proposes sending data between chips with beams of laser light — using vertical-cavity surface-emitting lasers (VCSELs), the same technology behind facial recognition in hundreds of millions of iPhones — eliminating the reach problem and potentially packing 220 memory chips around a single GPU.

CEO Tapa Ghosh says a chip could arrive next year to speed up workloads like AI coding. By reusing proven components rather than inventing new ones, Volantis is betting it can dodge the supply-chain bottlenecks that sink most ambitious chip startups — and it lands on the same day Micron confirmed the memory problem it promises to solve.

Saturday, September 26, 2026 — the 24 hours in AI hardware

Why it matters: The day belonged to the physical layer of AI. The Vera Rubin generation is now shipping hardware, Washington is deciding who may supply the optics connecting it, Intel is pitching its next foundry node to every major chipmaker, and Google is testing whether AI compute can leave the planet entirely. Underneath it all, the supply chain keeps repricing: memory, wafers, and AI servers are all getting more expensive — cost, not silicon, is becoming the buildout's real speed limit.

✦ Cover Story

Supermicro Ships First Vera Rubin NVL72 Liquid-Cooled Racks

Supermicro said on September 23 that it has started shipping NVIDIA Vera Rubin NVL72 racks bundled with its Data Center Building Block Solutions package and DLC-2 liquid cooling. Each rack combines 72 Rubin GPUs and 36 Vera CPUs across 18 one-unit compute trays and nine sixth-generation NVLink switch trays — with 20.7 TB of HBM4 and up to 54 TB of LPDDR5X per rack. The company's reference scalable unit links 16 compute racks for 1,152 GPUs as a single scale-out system.

Supermicro is selling more than a compute rack: the package includes cooling hardware, networking integration, cabling, storage, and deployment services. The company did not disclose shipment volumes, prices, named customers, or delivery schedules — so treat this as a vendor shipping update rather than proof of broad availability. Still, it marks the moment the Rubin generation moved from roadmaps and cloud announcements to real hardware leaving real loading docks.

04 — SPACE COMPUTE

Google's AI Chips Head to Orbit Next Week

Google said Thursday it will launch a prototype satellite carrying TPUs next week aboard SpaceX's Transporter-18 rideshare (in partnership with Planet Labs) — the first in-orbit test of Project Suncatcher, its research effort on space-based AI computing. Unlike synthetic leaderboard tests, it measures models on actual business workflows, making it a more practical signal for teams choosing a model for production coding. The ranking also sharpens the competitive picture: OpenAI's Astra leads on agentic benchmarks (tasks completed in roughly half the time of GPT-5.6 Sol, per recent reporting), while Claude holds the real-world-code crown — capability edges are increasingly workload-specific rather than universal.

Anthropic Launches Claude for Financial Advisors, Chasing OpenAI Into the Vertical Playbook

Anthropic launched Claude for Financial Advisors on September 14, wiring Claude into investment analytics and wealth-management software from BlackRock, Charles Schwab, and Addepar — plus data from Envestnet, iCapital, Orion, Wealthbox, Wealth.com, and Zocks — for client prep, portfolio review, and follow-up work. It arrives days after OpenAI introduced a finance-focused ChatGPT for investment bankers and equity researchers, and follows Anthropic's broader financial-services push around investment research and modeling. The vertical playbook is now the default frontier strategy: the same week OpenAI shipped a legal-industry Astra and Google expanded Gemini Enterprise for legal, the labs are racing to own professional workflows, not just chat windows.
