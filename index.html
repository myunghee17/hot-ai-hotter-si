Research Watch: Brain-Inspired “Post-CMOS” Chips for Lower-Power AI

Reported Oct 23 • alextech.ai

A scientific review from the University of Ottawa, Columbia, and Hanyang University (published in Advanced Functional Materials) surveys post-CMOS neurons — neuromorphic devices built from emerging materials beyond silicon — that physically reproduce how neurons sum incoming signals, co-locating memory and compute instead of shuttling data back and forth. The authors argue the approach could sharply cut energy versus conventional processors, for data centers and low-power wearables alike.

This is a research review, not a product: lab-to-fab scaling and compatibility with existing semiconductor processes remain open problems.

Why it matters: The same physics the energy-efficient-computing thesis bets on — material innovation toward lower-power compute — is quietly moving from lab reviews toward industrial attention.
