Samsung to Tap TSMC for NVIDIA's Next-Gen NVHBM Memory

Samsung Electronics will use TSMC's 3-nanometer process to produce the base dies for NVIDIA's next-generation custom HBM chips, the Seoul Economic Daily reported on Sunday, as carried by Reuters. NVIDIA has asked Samsung, SK Hynix, and Micron to build the NVHBM base dies on TSMC's process while using sixth-generation DRAM for the core dies. Samsung accepted NVIDIA's terms after an internal review but plans to keep its in-house production model for standard HBM; it is also weighing whether its own foundry could take over base-die production if demand outstrips TSMC capacity.

Micron Developing Edge Inference Memory (EIM) for Humanoid Robots

Micron is preparing edge inference memory (EIM), a memory semiconductor designed for physical AI — humanoid robots, autonomous driving, AR/VR — vice president Lee Cheol said at the "Semiconductors Powering Humanoids" conference at SEDEX 2026 in Seoul on October 16. EIM stacks low-power DRAM (LPDDR) on top of a logic die, sits between the AI chip and memory to cut latency and raise bandwidth, and differs from processing-in-memory (PIM) — PIM computes inside the memory itself, while EIM's compute architecture remains undisclosed. Micron is developing EIM on LPDDR6, with industry observers expecting mass production in the second half of 2028. Specific performance targets were not disclosed.

Why it matters: humanoids need to perceive, decide, and act in real time — a workload the classic CPU/GPU/memory hierarchy doesn't fit well. Micron is sketching a new memory tier purpose-built for embodied inference, and it lands exactly in the physical-AI lane where energy efficiency, not just raw throughput, decides the winners.
