Microsoft hands Intel's EMIB its first hyperscaler — and the OSAT queue overflows

Why it matters: This week the packaging bottleneck sprouted a second front. Microsoft became the first company to publicly commit its own silicon to Intel's EMIB bridges, giving Intel Foundry the hyperscaler packaging endorsement it has chased — and a second source for Maia's chiplet future. At the same time, TSMC's CoWoS queue overflowed into the merchant OSATs: ASE and Amkor are now booked for a projected 466,000 wafers of 2.5D packaging in 2027, a 5.5-fold jump. And the memory side of the package is already spoken for — all three HBM makers have most of their 2027 output under contract, while Huawei showed it is engineering its way around the HBM embargo with a custom hybrid-bonded stack. The package is no longer the bottleneck's waiting room; it is where the industry's money, standards and geopolitics now meet.

1st — announced EMIB customer for first-party silicon 466K wafers — 2027 OSAT 2.5D demand, a 5.5× jump ~30% — HBM's share of DRAM wafer capacity in 2027

COVER STORY

Microsoft picks Intel EMIB to package future Azure silicon

Microsoft and Intel announced on November 18 an expanded long-term agreement that makes Microsoft the first company to announce use of Intel Foundry's Embedded Multi-die Interconnect Bridge (EMIB) advanced packaging for Azure first-party silicon. The release names no chip, process node, volume or date — but the logic is visible in Microsoft's own roadmap. Maia 200, launched in January, is a monolithic TSMC N3P die near the reticle limit with 216 GB of HBM3E; Maia 300, unveiled at the OCP Global Summit in October, splits compute, memory-controller and I/O functions into separate chiplets, moves compute to TSMC N2P, and feeds it 288 GB of HBM4 at 18 TB/s per accelerator. Microsoft plans multi-gigawatt Maia deployments starting in 2027, with Maia as its primary inference system. A gigawatt plan is a packaging plan — and a second qualified packaging source removes one schedule risk Microsoft controls directly.

The agreement also confirms multi-year Xeon supply capacity, including Azure instances of the next-generation server CPU codenamed Diamond Rapids, plus joint work on CXL memory, Intel QuickAssist Technology and confidential computing. On the CPU side, Microsoft made Cobalt 200 VMs generally available at Ignite on November 17, built on Arm Neoverse CSS V3 with up to 50% better performance than Cobalt 100 by Microsoft's measure — and a bridge-based package lets a future Cobalt place different core classes on different dies in one socket.

Futurum's chip-library model sizes the stakes: Maia 200 is 2.0 reticles per package with $0.67K of advanced packaging per unit, about 6.5% of a $10.4K silicon ASP; Maia 300 grows to 3.5 reticles and $1.25K of packaging per unit, about 8.3% of a $15.2K ASP — each Maia 300 consuming roughly 1.9× the interposer wafer area of its predecessor. The model puts Intel EMIB at 11,000 wafers per month in 2026 rising to 35,000 in 2028, against TSMC CoWoS growing from 140,000 to 245,000 wafers per month over the same period. Intel Foundry data cited by Futurum puts EMIB wafer utilization near 90%, against roughly 60% for an 8× reticle RDL interposer. The caveats are real: no named chip, node, date or volume; EMIB-T with HBM4 has yet to ship at production volume for an outside XPU; and Futurum expects the first EMIB-packaged Maia to follow Maia 300, with Microsoft likely running EMIB as a second source alongside TSMC. Intel's packaging business gains a reference customer today. Revenue arrives only when a named Maia or Cobalt generation ships on EMIB-T at volume.

Source: Futurum Group, Nov. 23 · Source: Microsoft, Nov. 18

The spillover goes merchant: ASE and Amkor book 466,000 wafers of 2.5D packaging

TSMC's CoWoS lines are filled through 2026, and the overflow now has a number: outsourced 2.5D packaging demand across ASE and Amkor is projected at 466,000 wafers in 2027, a 5.5-fold increase over 2026 levels, according to industry data reported November 26. Global shipments of high-end cloud AI accelerators are estimated at 26 million units in 2027, with TSMC expected to package 85.8% of them — its CoWoS-L variant taking 54.6% of total accelerator shipments — and the rest spilling to merchant floors. ASE is moving fastest, on track to expand its CoWoS-like capacity to 45,000 wafers per month by the end of 2027 on its proprietary FOCoS-Bridge platform; Amkor is targeting 20,000 wafers per month by late 2027 for its comparable S-Connect architecture. Together the two would account for virtually all merchant 2.5D wafer handling for tier-one cloud accelerators. AMD's Venice and Nvidia's Vera are named as the server platforms leaning most heavily on outsourced 2.5D capacity, and contract pricing for outsourced 2.5D assembly has climbed 15% to 20% over standard packaging rates, which cloud providers are absorbing to hit deployment targets. Samsung is expanding its I-Cube lines to capture overflow, while JCET and Tongfu lack high-density interposer licenses for leading-edge nodes, and Ibiden and Shinko face multi-year backlogs for large-body ABF substrates. The figures are industry supply-chain estimates — the companies have not disclosed confirmed order allocations — but the direction is unambiguous: the merchant OSAT is now a first-class citizen in AI packaging, not a relief valve.

Source: East Asia Brief, Nov. 26 (industry data; figures are supply-chain estimates)

Packaging is no longer the cheap afterthought after fabrication; it is getting front-end-sized capital.

HBM4's 2027 output is already mostly spoken for

The three companies that make high-bandwidth memory have already committed most of their 2027 output, and the capacity expansion each is now paying for mostly comes online in 2027 or later. Samsung said on its October 29 earnings call that its 2027 HBM supply "is already sold out," with HBM4 about 60% of its HBM revenue and more than tripled quarter on quarter; it has signed long-term agreements with 10 major customers, is negotiating with 10 more, and aims to cover 60% to 70% of its production capacity with such agreements by 2030. SK hynix said long-term agreements with 14 customers cover more than half of its production capacity for 2027 and 2028, expects HBM4 to pass HBM3E in shipment volume and revenue in the fourth quarter, will run its M15X facility at maximum capacity by year-end, and expects demand to exceed supply through 2030. Micron said it raised calendar-2027 HBM pricing "significantly," with more than 75% of fiscal 2027 shipments already under customer allocation or contract; its Singapore HBM facility ramps in 2027, first wafer output from Idaho arrives mid-2027, and most of its added construction capex goes to cleanrooms that come online in late 2028 and beyond. The industry-level figure: HBM is expected to take nearly 30% of the DRAM industry's wafer capacity in 2027, up from about 20%, according to Samsung EVP Kim Taewoo via Reuters and TrendForce — and because HBM and conventional DRAM are made on the same wafers, that share comes directly out of DDR4, DDR5 and LPDDR supply. TrendForce projects HBM bit shipments up 50% to 60% year over year in 2027, with the blended average selling price jumping 121%. Micron's verdict on when the market balances: "we really don't have line of sight." One more shift to watch: Google is reportedly looking to buy HBM directly for AI chips developed with MediaTek, moving away from Broadcom's turnkey model — which Counterpoint estimates adds a 15% to 20% markup to HBM costs. That report is unconfirmed, and none of the companies involved has commented.

05

Huawei's custom HBM doubles the interface — on paper

Huawei will present a custom high-bandwidth memory design at ISSCC 2027 in San Francisco (February 14–18) whose paper title claims double the data-pin density of the memory Samsung and SK hynix currently mass-produce, Bloomberg reported November 26. The paper, "A 64-Channel 4096-DQ Custom HBM with Memory Controllers and 5-Layer Wafer-on-Wafer Hybrid Bonding," describes a 4096-bit interface — twice the 2048-bit width of the HBM4 standard — across 64 channels, with the memory controller integrated directly into the stack and five wafer layers joined by wafer-on-wafer hybrid bonding, a 3D packaging method that bonds copper directly rather than relying on solder bumps. It is one of four Huawei papers in the ISSCC 2027 program, alongside logic 3D stacking, a 33A 3D-stacked voltage regulator the title says cuts system power by 59W for AI processors, and high-speed wireline work; ChangXin Memory (CXMT) will also present a 32Gb DDR5 design at 9.2 Gb/s per pin using wafer-to-wafer hybrid copper bonding. The context is sanctions: Huawei recently asked Ascend 960DT customers to source their own HBM, and its next Ascend accelerator is still expected to run largely on Korean memory acquired through unofficial channels, according to reports. The caution is in the evidence itself — a paper title discloses no capacity, no per-pin speed and no production timeline, and Bloomberg hedged that "how far the design has been put into practice is unclear." ISSCC papers report measured silicon, so the February presentation will show whether the 4096-DQ stack is a working device or a design study. Either way, it is the clearest sign yet that Huawei's answer to the HBM embargo runs through hybrid bonding, not around it.

Source: EE Times, Nov. 26 (Bloomberg) · Source: TrendForce, Nov. 26

AaltoSemi files for an A-share IPO, riding the substrate shortage

Nanjing-based AaltoSemi, a maker of advanced IC packaging substrates for AI and other high-performance chips, has started the process for an initial public offering in China. The week's numbers: what a package actually is

A November 22 3D explainer from Thorsten Meyer AI lays out the anatomy of a modern AI accelerator package, bottom up: package substrate, silicon interposer, compute dies, HBM stacks, cooling plate. The numbers in it are a useful scale check. TSMC's CoWoS-S supports interposers up to about 2,700 square millimeters — 3.3 times a standard exposure reticle — with 3.5-reticle designs in volume production since 2024; TSMC's CEO said in October the company was in "real world production" of five-reticle designs and working on nine- and 14-reticle formats. Per-GPU memory bandwidth has risen from 900 GB/s on the V100 to 19.2 TB/s on Rubin, which uses two reticle-limited compute dies; AMD's MI350 uses eight vertically stacked accelerator dies and two I/O dies, and the MI355X carries eight 12-high HBM stacks. And the demand backdrop behind all of it: SK hynix expects HBM demand to exceed supply through 2030. When the package is 3.3 reticles wide and the memory on it is sold out for a year, the package is the product.

Nvidia's $390B supply bet turns packaging into the purchase order

Why it matters: This week the bottleneck got a buyer. Nvidia's first post-Huang earnings didn't just beat expectations — it converted the packaging shortage into signed, funded, dated capacity: SPIL tripling its advanced packaging output by mid-2029, Kinsus expanding substrate supply more than 3.5×, and Amkor's $3 billion build-out. Meanwhile Yole Group says advanced packaging revenue overtakes traditional packaging for the first time in 2026, 16-layer HBM4 started shipping for Vera Rubin, and the chiplet world kept standardizing — an imec interposer design kit, an automotive chiplet rulebook, and AI models learning the atomic physics of hybrid bonding. The transistor era's bottleneck is now a line item on the world's biggest balance sheet.

$390B — Nvidia supply commitments 3.5× — Kinsus substrate expansion mid-2029 — SPIL capacity tripling lands

COVER STORY

Nvidia signs the packaging industry's biggest shopping list

On its November 17 earnings call — the first since Jensen Huang's resignation — interim CEO Colette Kress announced new substrate agreements with Kinsus and Samsung Electro-Mechanics and a new advanced packaging agreement with SPIL, telling investors "we've secured memory, we've secured advanced packaging." Nvidia then spelled out the deals: Kinsus will expand ABF substrate capacity dedicated to Nvidia by more than 3.5× at its Youth campus in Taoyuan, with production starting in the second half of 2027 and added capacity targeted for Q3 2028; Samsung Electro-Mechanics amended its existing substrate agreement to lift output at current factories; and SPIL will triple its advanced packaging capacity in Taiwan by mid-2029 under a multiyear agreement backed by multibillion-dollar Nvidia funding, covering bumping, interposer wafer fabrication, assembly and testing. The deals sit alongside nearly $3 billion in multiyear agreements already supporting Amkor's expansion in Arizona and South Korea. The context is a $115.8 billion quarter — data center revenue $107.6 billion, Vera Rubin contributing about $20 billion in its first quarter of production — and supply commitments that jumped $111 billion in one quarter to $390 billion, driven mainly by multiyear memory procurement. Kress also said SK hynix supplies about 70% of the HBM4 for Vera Rubin. None of the new capacity arrives before 2027, so the shortage persists into next year — but for the first time, the fix has names, dates and prepayments attached.

02 — The $28B year: advanced packaging revenue overtakes traditional packaging

Yole Group expects capital spending on advanced packaging to near $28 billion in 2026 — the first year advanced packaging revenue overtakes revenue from traditional packaging, Reuters reported November 6 after interviews with more than a dozen industry executives and analysts. The bottleneck has officially become the industry's biggest growth market.

03 — SK hynix ships 16-layer HBM4 for Rubin as the bandwidth bar settles at 19.2 TB/s

The memory stack is no longer the experiment — it is the allocation fight.

04 — Axelera's Titania: a chiplet package that dodges HBM entirely

Axelera AI unveiled Titania on November 12, its third-generation inference architecture built as a chiplet-based system-in-package — and its most packaging-relevant decision is what it leaves out. But the design bet is clear — when HBM and CoWoS are the queue, a package built on LPDDR6 and UCIe is a way around the line.

05 — imec's NanoIC interposer baseline opens Europe's chiplet door

Imec announced at SEMICON Europa 2026 on November 10 that it has delivered the baseline flows for the silicon interposer platform inside its NanoIC pilot line — the manufacturing reference for a dedicated process design kit (PDK) scheduled for release in early 2027. The platform captures through-silicon vias, multiple metal routing layers and support for microbump pitches down to 20 µm in a 2.5D package, letting universities, start-ups and fabless companies explore interposer designs in Cadence and Synopsys environments before committing to fabrication. The interposer PDK will complement NanoIC's already-available PDKs for fine-pitch RDL interposers and die-to-wafer hybrid bonding, with low-volume prototyping to follow. Imec is publishing the recipe rather than building the factory: the point is a common, accessible technology foundation so European teams can build expertise in chiplet integration for AI, automotive and physical-AI systems without waiting on a foundry's roadmap. It is the week's quietest announcement — and the one most likely to widen who gets to build chiplets at all.

Source: imec, Nov. 10 · Source: Bits&Chips, Nov. 10

06 — Carmakers get a chiplet rulebook: the ACIR standard

Imec and Japan's Advanced SoC Research for Automotive (ASRA) released the Automotive Chiplet Interoperability Requirements (ACIR) on November 12, unveiled at the Autonomous Edge Chiplet Forum in Japan. The document sets a common set of architectural rules for automotive chiplets — error and system management, boot sequencing, power management, functional safety and die-to-die connectivity — including a shared error-reporting infrastructure so faults can be handled with safe, predictable decisions. The first version is publicly downloadable for industry feedback and states what interoperable chiplets must support without prescribing how vendors implement it; it complements existing automotive chiplet standards rather than replacing them. It lands less than a year after the two organisations announced strategic alignment, and imec's Automotive Chiplet Program has since become the Autonomous Edge Chiplet Program. Cars are the next big chiplet market — driver assistance, autonomous driving and intelligent cockpits all push monolithic SoCs past their economic limit — and ACIR is the attempt to make sure chiplets from different suppliers actually work together when safety is on the line.

Source: MooreCharm, Nov. 14 (imec/ASRA release) · Source: Silicon Feeds, week of Nov. 9

08 — imec and Matlantis teach AI the atomic physics of hybrid bonding

Imec signed a Joint Development Project with Matlantis on November 14 to simulate microchip bonding at atomic scale, using machine learning to attack one of 3D integration's stubbornest bottlenecks: predicting whether a hybrid bond will actually stick. The project, called SIMPL (Simulations with Machine-Learning Potentials), applies Matlantis's Preferred Potential — a universal machine-learning interatomic potential — to the boundary interfaces where amorphous dielectrics meet polycrystalline metals, the exact surfaces that decide hybrid-bond adhesion. It is led by imec Fellow Geoffrey Pourtois and Matlantis CEO Daisuke Okanohara, and the aim is a predictive framework that replaces empirical trial-and-error in advanced packaging. Hybrid bonding already works in production — TSMC has scaled SoIC bond pitch from 9 to 6 microns, Intel ships Foveros Direct, AMD has used it in volume since the first 3D V-Cache parts — but every pitch shrink multiplies the surface-physics unknowns. If atomistic simulation can predict bond quality before a wafer is run, the yield learning curve for sub-5-micron bonds gets a lot shorter.

Source: Silicon Polder, Nov. 14

09 — Hybrid bonding's next wall is metrology, not the bond itself

As hybrid-bond pitches fall below 5 µm, the failure mode moves from the bond to the inspection: particle contamination and nanometer-scale topography become critical, and the tools that must catch them — optical, AFM, IR, X-ray and acoustic imaging — are being stretched to detect sub-100 nm defects both before and after bonding, Silicon Feeds' week-of-November-9 brief reports. The pitch roadmap makes the problem concrete: TSMC has scaled its SoIC bond pitch from 9 microns to 6 and laid out a path to 4.5 by 2029, while imec and EV Group have demonstrated 200-nanometer wafer-to-wafer pitch in research and CEA-Leti has shown 1-micron die-to-wafer pitch. Each shrink multiplies the number of vertical connections between stacked dies — face-to-face hybrid bonding reaches around 14,000 signals per square millimeter against roughly 1,500 for face-to-back TSV stacking — but only if the bond is clean. Samsung's own packaging executives flagged the same point at ISMP 2026: controlling particle contamination and preventing void formation remain the major challenges for its 3D Cube-H hybrid-bonded stacking. The bond works; proving it worked, at volume, is now the bottleneck.

The chiplet standard just grew up — UCIe 4.0 puts memory inside the package

Why it matters: This week the package became the system. UCIe 4.0 standardized the memory link between chiplets, turning the interconnect standard into a system-level spec. TSMC locked in another seven years of CoWoS capacity through Xintec, Samsung broke ground on an HBM packaging plant a full year early, and glass substrates moved from pilot talk to paid samples and equipment orders. The bottleneck is no longer the transistor — it is the interconnect, the interposer, and the substrate, and this week's money followed all three.

93.75% — UCIe.mem data efficiency 2029 — Xintec CoWoS line ramps ₩6T — Samsung HBM plant build

COVER STORY

UCIe 4.0 turns the chiplet standard from wiring into a system

The UCIe Consortium released the UCIe 4.0 specification on November 12, moving the chiplet interconnect standard from die-to-die connectivity toward full system-level integration. The headline addition is UCIe.mem, the first standardized on-package memory link between compute dies and memory logic dies, working with existing memory technologies such as HBM and LPDDR — it lets memory control be separated from the compute die. The spec claims up to 93.75% data efficiency and roughly 2.5 TB/s of bandwidth density per millimeter of die edge, with co-optimized link profiles cutting link power by 30% to 80% under specified conditions (consortium figures, for the die-to-die link — not system-wide power). It also adds TRoCs (Trusted Reporting of Chiplet State) for integrity-protected reporting of die health, fault notification even when the normal communication path is down, and automotive reliability features. The specification is fully backward compatible with all earlier UCIe versions, and ships with a white paper from consortium chairman Debendra Das Sharma of Intel. No product timing was given — the numbers are consortium targets until the first commercial implementations arrive — but the direction is clear: the memory wall is now being attacked inside the package, not just at the board.

Source: HPCwire, Nov. 12 · Source: XenoSpectrum, Nov. 13
